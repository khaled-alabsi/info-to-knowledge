# Terms this team uses

The transcript is made by speech recognition, and it writes a term the team uses as a word that sounds like it. Below
are the terms, each with what it means. When a word in the transcript does not fit what is being said, and one of these
terms fits it, reason from the context: the slide, the code, the sentence around it. Write the term as it is listed
here only when the context supports it, and otherwise keep the word and note it as unclear. A word that only sounds
like a term is not that term by itself. This list is for words no screen shows: a word the screen spells (a title, a
label, a product's name) is written as the screen spells it, listed here or not, and the transcript's spelling of it is
no mistake to keep.

**When you are checking text that is already written, go through this list every time before you decide the text
is right.** That is the second look at a batch's notes and the review of the document: take each term below, look for
the words it says the transcript writes for it, and search the text for those words. A reader who only reads the text
and waits for a word to look wrong finds none of them, because every one of them is an ordinary word. Where such a
word stands and the context supports the term, the word is wrong, and correcting it is part of your job, in the shape
your own instruction gives. Where the context does not support the term, leave the word and say it is unclear.

- **Click dummy**: a clickable mock-up of a user interface, with no logic behind it, used to show and test a design.
  The transcript writes it "Qlik dummy", which is not the Qlik analytics product.
- **girocard**: the German debit card scheme, beside the international schemes such as Visa; a card can carry both.
  The transcript writes it "Zero-Card" or "Zero-Karten".
- **Legal**: the bank's legal department, which decides on legal requirements such as a customer's consent. The
  transcript writes it "Lidl", which is not the retailer.
- **Vorsorge**: German for provision, the business area of pension and insurance cover; it names the platform the team builds for it. The transcript writes it "ForSorger" and "for Sorge".
- **BA**: business analyst, the role that writes requirements and test cases. "BA capacity" is the business
  analysts' working time.
- **PO**: always the Product Owner, the role that decides what a team builds and in which order. A slide or a speaker
  writing "PO" means that role; never an open question.
- **Keycloak**: the open-source identity and access server, an example of an authorization server that issues tokens.
  The transcript writes it "Keyclock", and a speaker's German "der Keycloak" can read "de Keycloak"; neither is a typo
  to ask about.
- **Claude Code**: Anthropic's coding agent, run in a terminal, whose screen shows its name. The transcript writes it
  "Cloud Code", which is no product of its own.
